One 100-word monologue (105 words, 562 characters) spoken by Vess Marrow, witch-queen of the Salt Marsh — a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired:

> Ah. You again. I am Vess Marrow, witch-queen of the Salt Marsh, and you kneel in my mud, sword shaking, same speech your father gave me — though I stopped listening around 'justice.' Sit. The tide is in, the drowned sailors are singing, and I have a harbour to flood. My sister's ghost keeps the ledger. I keep... what do I keep? The plan was simple: take the lighthouse, drown the fleet, crown myself queen of the black water. Only — the candles are low. The stairs are many. And you brought a torch. How thoughtful. The marsh is patient. I am not. Not anymore.

Vess never says she is weary. The tiredness is in the pauses, the trailing off, the small deflections — "Sit", "what do I keep?", "How thoughtful" — and in a scheme that is still simple but somehow too much: the candles are low, the stairs are many. The sting lands in the last line: the marsh is patient, and she is not. Not anymore.
